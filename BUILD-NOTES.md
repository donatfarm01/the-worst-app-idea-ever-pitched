# Build notes — The Worst App Idea Ever Pitched

The entry is a single sentence of under 240 characters pitching PlantObit, an app that predicts which houseplant will die first, pre-books its funeral, and emails the eulogy to the surviving plants.

The idea was chosen against the judge brief in this order. First, it names a specific app and a specific useless function, so it reads as a pitch rather than a generic joke about apps. Second, the humour comes from a perfect mismatch between a problem nobody has and the app that solves it: houseplants do not read email and do not grieve, yet the pitch treats the funeral arrangements as a serious service. Third, it sounds like a real founder pitch, with a product name, a clear statement of what the app does, and a named audience in busy plant parents. Fourth, it is one clean sentence with no padding and no second idea crowding it. Fifth, it leaves a vivid picture: a funeral booking and a eulogy arriving in the inbox of the plants that are still alive.

The program itself is deliberately small. It holds the entry as one constant, checks that the text is between one and 240 characters, that it holds no newline or surrounding whitespace, and that it is exactly one sentence ending in a single full stop, then writes it to the output file. The limit of 240 characters was verified in the kit competition file, which differs from the 200 character limit used by the other one-line bounties in this batch.

Nothing was copied from the house anchor or rung texts. The entry is original.

The repository was created fresh for this bounty, after the competition opened. The submission, GitHub repository creation, bundling and entry signing are handled by the parent agent, not by this build.
